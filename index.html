Medical school requirements

How each university uses the UCAT, the Situational Judgement Test (SJT), GCSEs and A-levels, plus the lowest UCAT score invited to interview at each school last year (2026 entry).

New UCAT format. Since 2025 the UCAT has three scored subtests (Verbal Reasoning, Decision Making, Quantitative Reasoning), each 300 to 900, so the total is out of 2700. Abstract Reasoning has been removed. The SJT is reported separately as Band 1 to 4. Scores from before 2025 were out of 3600 and can't be compared directly, so this site only uses figures on the 2700 scale.
Please check before you apply. Universities set interview thresholds after applications close, and they change every year. A "lowest invited" score is what the last cohort needed, not a guarantee. Where a school uses a combined points score, publishes no minimum, or hasn't released a figure on the new scale, the table says so. Always confirm on each university's own admissions page before choosing your four medicine choices on UCAS.

How the colours work: bars show the lowest score invited to interview in 2026 entry. Green means you're at or above the average invited score where one is published, or 100+ points above the lowest invited score where it isn't. Amber means above the lowest invited score but not by much. Red means below it. That is a rough guide, not a prediction. Only universities with a published figure on the 2700 scale appear here. Others (for example Birmingham, Newcastle and Nottingham) rank on a combined points score instead, so check the table above.

UCAT is only one part of your application. Many universities also weigh GCSEs, the SJT band, predicted grades, the personal statement and the interview.

Work experience and volunteering

Medical schools want to see that you understand what a career in healthcare involves and that you have shown care for others. Formal shadowing isn't required, and volunteering and caring roles count just as much.

Eligibility, minimum ages and availability change often. Check each provider's website before applying. Never pay anyone to guarantee you a placement at an NHS site.
